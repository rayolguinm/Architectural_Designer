J a c a r a n d a

Olguin Architecture · Social House with Mixed Use · 1,300 SqFt

Jacaranda reinterprets the social-housing typology as a compact mixed-use system in which commerce, domestic life, climate response, and urban frontage are integrated within a repeatable architectural module. The ground floor activates the street through neighborhood retail, while the residential program is elevated to preserve privacy and establish a clear gradient between public, semi-private, and domestic space. The façade operates as an environmental filter rather than a conventional enclosure. Perforated masonry screens modulate solar radiation, ventilation, visibility, and daylight while giving the development a coherent tectonic identity. A rational structural grid, compact footprint, and repetitive construction logic support material efficiency and scalability, demonstrating how modest residential architecture can achieve greater spatial performance through passive design, programmatic density, and precise control of the building envelope.

C l u b H o u s e

Olguin Architecture · Arch Viz · Commercial Design · 32,000 SqFt

Club House is conceived as a planetary science and NASA research facility organized around a large-span horizontal structure that integrates laboratories, collaborative workspaces, visualization areas, and public-facing scientific programs. The elevated volume creates a continuous research platform while preserving an open ground plane for circulation, landscape, outreach, and shared gathering spaces. Structurally, the building relies on a disciplined long-span framing system with concentrated vertical supports, allowing flexible interior planning and large unobstructed zones suitable for multidisciplinary scientific work. The architecture is shaped by the operational needs of planetary research: modular organization, clear circulation, visual connectivity, controlled daylight, and adaptable technical spaces. The upper terrace functions as an observation and collaboration platform oriented toward the horizon, while the landscape below extends the research environment into the site. Solar exposure, thermal performance, environmental monitoring, and topographic integration inform the design, creating a facility where architecture supports scientific investigation, mission collaboration, data visualization, and planetary exploration research.

T C a b i n

Olguin Architecture · Arch Viz · Residential

T Cabin is organized around a dramatic roof geometry that defines both the architectural identity and structural logic of the residence. Two inclined roof planes extend beyond the enclosure, creating deep overhangs that protect the glazed façades while emphasizing vertical space at the center of the plan. The structural concept suggests a rigid frame concentrated along the primary walls and roof junctions, allowing the perimeter to remain highly transparent and the interior volume visually continuous. The double-height enclosure strengthens the relationship between interior and exterior, while the pool establishes a linear axis that extends the architecture into the landscape. Timber soffits, concrete surfaces, and dark structural elements differentiate enclosure from support and reinforce the roof as the dominant tectonic feature. From a planetary-science perspective, the project explores solar orientation, thermal protection, gravity, water, and environmental exposure, treating the building envelope as a responsive system shaped by the forces acting on its site.

L H o u s e

Olguin Architecture · Arch Viz · Residential

L House is conceived as an architectural composition where structure, material, and terrain operate as a single system. A heavy stone-clad base establishes the residence as a grounded element within the desert landscape, while the upper volume is expressed as a precise cantilever that shifts the visual center of gravity and creates protected space below. The structural concept is organized around a rigid load-bearing frame and transfer zone capable of supporting the projected mass while allowing long spans, open-plan interiors, and extensive glazing at the ground level. The L-shaped configuration defines a controlled microclimate around the courtyard and pool, using the building mass itself to generate shade, privacy, and spatial enclosure. Deep overhangs, recessed façades, and vertical timber screens respond to solar exposure while framing views toward the surrounding vegetation and distant topography. From a planetary-science perspective, the project can be read as a study of mass, gravity, solar orientation, thermal exposure, and terrain interaction—principles that also govern how natural landforms and built systems respond to their physical environment. The result is an architecture that appears simultaneously anchored to the ground and suspended above it.
